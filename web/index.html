Privacy, in plain words

The honest version, no fine print.

Here's how Haven keeps your life yours — what stays private, and the honest limits too.

What stays private

  • Every post, message, photo, video and call is encrypted on your device before it's sent.
  • The secret keys that unlock your content stay on your own devices. We never hold them.
  • There's no central account, no profile database, no advertising ID, no analytics.
  • Choose your own storage — a relay you run, or your own S3 bucket. We run no storage and hold no credentials.
Where the keys actually live›

In the Secure Enclave on Apple devices, the OS secure store elsewhere. The only way they sync between your devices is Apple's end-to-end-encrypted iCloud Keychain, which we can't read either.

“Your own storage” means a Haven relay you (or a friend) run, or your own S3 bucket (S3/R2/B2) — we hold no credentials for any of it.

The honest limits

  • A relay (an optional helper) can see that some encrypted bytes passed through, but never what they say.
  • To deliver bytes to you, a relay briefly handles your IP address — never logged, never stored, and never tied to a name, because there isn't one.
  • Friends never see each other's IP when a relay is in the path — only the relay does, briefly.
  • If someone steals your unlocked, malware-infected device, they have your device. No app on earth fixes that.
What that means for your IP›

A relay handling your IP for a moment is just how the internet works — every app you own does the same thing. The difference is that Haven's relay never logs it, never stores it, and has nothing to tie it to: no account, no name, no email, no phone.

Being precise: while a relay is moving your bytes it does see your app's key — a long random identifier — alongside your IP. It has to check that key, or a stranger could help themselves to your circle's mailbox. It can't read anything with it, and it can't turn it into your name. So the promise is never logged, never sold, never readable — not "never seen".

If you want to hide your IP from a relay entirely, run Haven behind your own VPN, or host your own relay. We looked hard at a built-in onion/Tor mode and decided against it: Tor can't carry the kind of traffic Haven's calls and direct connections need, so it would quietly break them.

New in 1.0.7 · Security & polish

Harder to break into, easier to tidy up.

The big security release. It quietly upgrades how your devices trust each other and how your storage stays under control — automatically, circle by circle, as everyone updates. Nothing to set up, nothing lost.

A stolen device can now be locked out for real

  • A device you link no longer has to hold a copy of your master key — each one runs on its own keys.
  • So removing a lost or stolen device can cut it off cryptographically — from your circle's content and from the settings and contacts that sync between your devices — not just hide it.
  • Your master key stays on one primary device plus your encrypted iCloud Keychain backup, instead of being copied everywhere.
  • Everything is still hybrid post-quantum — built to resist tomorrow's quantum computers, today.
How the rollout works, honestly›

This turns on per circle, once every member's devices have updated — an all-present signal, never guessed from silence. Until then a coexistence path keeps un-upgraded devices working exactly as before, so no one is ever stranded and no old message becomes unreadable.

One honest limit stays honest: this shrinks the key's footprint from every device to a single primary you choose. A fully compromised primary is still a full compromise — the fix for that is rolling a new identity, same as before.

Storage that stays under control

  • See what's taking space — a cleanup screen lists everything you've downloaded, sorted by size.
  • Delete to free space, and the post stays with a placeholder you can re-download anytime — nothing is really lost.
  • "Keep on this device" pins anything you never want swept away.
  • Set limits by age or size, on your device and on a relay you run — and auto-delete now really removes the media, not just the post.
And a little more polish›

Video is compressed on posting to save space and data. Story styling — including how photos and video are framed — now looks the same on every platform. And we closed a location-metadata leak on video, so nothing about where a clip was taken rides along.

Also shipping, out in the open: the next generation of Haven's group encryption — forward secrecy and post-compromise security, on our own post-quantum cryptography (MLS-style, not a classical off-the-shelf standard). It's enabled in this release and turns on per circle once everyone's devices have updated and joined — until then a circle stays on the existing key path, and a device that falls behind reverts within one sync, so no one is ever stranded. It only ever changes which key seals your content, never whether it's encrypted. We ran our own adversarial audit first — no critical or high findings, two mediums fixed — but that's our homework, not an outside review; an independent cryptographer's review is planned. Want it even more reliable?

Be the bridge for your circle.

Every Haven app can be a relay: flip “Be your circle's relay” in Settings and your device quietly holds your circle's sealed messages for friends who are offline. No terminal, nothing to install.

What your device is actually doing›

It runs reliably in the background, holding messages that stay end-to-end encrypted with post-quantum cryptography it can never open — your device is a post box, not a reader.

Leave a Mac or desktop app running and your whole circle gets more resilient.

Turn yours on

1. Open Haven on a device you leave running
2. Settings ▸ Relays → “Be your circle's relay”
3. Done — friends' apps find it automatically

Prefer a terminal? The standalone relay 
curl -fsSL https://wemiller.com/apps/haven/relay/install.sh | sh
haven-relay run --link "haven-relay://circle#…"

One command to install, one to go live — for a Pi or server with no Haven app. No ports, no domain, no cloud required.
